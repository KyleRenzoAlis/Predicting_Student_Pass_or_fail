A high-risk result is a reason to offer help, such as tutoring or a conversation. It is not a reason to sanction a student.

What the model learned

  • The standing after prefinal carries most of the signal, then the standing after midterm. Absences add a little.
  • In the full study, background facts such as family and lifestyle barely beat guessing, which is why this tool leaves them out.
  • The simplest model matched or beat the more complex ones, and it is the easiest to explain.

Use it carefully

  • Data comes from two Portuguese schools in 2005–2006, graded 0–20. Your scores are averaged with your weights, then converted to that scale with the passing mark matched (75 or 3.0 equals 10). The model learned from first- and second-period grades, so it treats your standing after midterm and prefinal that way. It has not been validated on Philippine class records.
  • The prediction sees only grades and absences. It cannot see effort, health or circumstances, so treat it as a reason to talk with the student.
  • Fewer than 30 failing students sit in each test split, so group comparisons are not reliable.
